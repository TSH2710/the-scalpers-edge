# R-Multiples Explained: The Only Metric That Matters for Scalpers

*Stop obsessing over win rate. Start tracking R-multiples. This is the metric that predicts whether you'll be profitable — and almost no scalpers track it.*

---

I'm going to make a claim that might sound crazy:

**Your win rate doesn't matter.**

Not in the way you think it does, anyway. A 40% win rate can make you rich. A 70% win rate can make you broke.

The metric that actually matters? **R-multiples.**

If you only learn one thing from this article, let it be this: start tracking R-multiples today.

## What Is an R-Multiple?

R stands for "risk." An R-multiple tells you how much you made (or lost) relative to how much you risked on the trade.

**The formula:**

```
R = (Exit Price - Entry Price) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss)
```

Let me break this down with a real example.

## Example: A Day of Scalping

Let's say you're scalping XYZ at $50/share.

**Trade 1 — The Winner:**
- Entry: $50.00
- Stop Loss: $49.80 (20 cent risk per share)
- Target: $50.60 (60 cent gain = 3R target)
- You hit target and exit at $50.60
- Position size: 200 shares
- R-multiple: ($50.60 - $50.00) / ($50.00 - $49.80) = 3R ✅

**Trade 2 — The Quick Exit:**
- Entry: $50.80
- Stop Loss: $50.60
- Target: $51.20
- You get scared and exit at $50.95 (15 cents)
- R-multiple: ($50.95 - $50.80) / ($50.80 - $50.60) = 0.75R ✅

**Trade 3 — The Loss:**
- Entry: $51.10
- Stop Loss: $50.90
- You get stopped out at $50.90
- R-multiple: ($50.90 - $51.10) / ($51.10 - $50.90) = -1R ❌

**Trade 4 — The Revenge Trade:**
- Entry: $51.00
- Stop Loss: $50.80
- You chase it down to $50.85 and stop out
- R-multiple: ($50.85 - $51.00) / ($51.00 - $50.80) = -0.75R ❌

**Trade 5 — The Recovery:**
- Entry: $50.70
- Stop Loss: $50.50
- Target: $51.10
- You hit target
- R-multiple: ($51.10 - $50.70) / ($50.70 - $50.50) = 2R ✅

## The Math

Let's add up the day:

| Trade | R-Multiple | P&L (200 shares) |
|-------|------------|------------------|
| 1 | +3R | +$120 |
| 2 | +0.75R | +$30 |
| 3 | -1R | -$40 |
| 4 | -0.75R | -$30 |
| 5 | +2R | +$80 |

**Net R for the day: +4R**
**Net P&L: +$160**

**Win rate: 3 out of 5 = 60%.**

That looks decent. But here's the thing — the win rate didn't tell the whole story.

What if Trade 2 had been -0.5R instead of +0.75R? Now you're at +2.75R instead of +4R. Same win rate, completely different result.

What if Trade 5 was only +0.5R? Now you're at +2.5R. Still positive, but barely.

What if you took 15 trades instead of 5 and had the same distribution? The R-multiples compound. Your average R stays the same. Your net result grows (or shrinks) proportionally.

**Win rate is a distraction. R-multiple is the truth.**

## The Magic Number: Average R = 1.0

Here's the benchmark:

**If your average R-multiple is above 1.0, your strategy is profitable.**

This is the number that matters. Here's why:

Let's say you trade 100 times with an average R of 1.1.
- Even if your win rate is only 40%
- Even if you have 5-trade losing streaks
- Even if your best trade is only 1.5R

You're profitable. Net result: +110R.

Now flip it: average R of 0.8, even with a 60% win rate.
- 60 wins at 0.8R = +48R
- 40 losses at -1R = -40R
- Net: +8R

But your 0.8R means your winners are SMALLER than your losses. A single bad streak can wipe you out. With 1.1R, your winners are bigger than your losses. You can survive bad streaks.

## My First Month of Tracking R-Multiples

I started tracking R-multiples about 5 months into my trading. Here's what I found:

**Month 1:**
- Win rate: 42%
- Average R: 1.4R
- Net result: +16R (profitable despite low win rate)

**Month 2:**
- Win rate: 51%
- Average R: 0.9R
- Net result: +3.6R (barely profitable, and felt like a grind)

**Month 3:**
- Win rate: 38%
- Average R: 1.6R
- Net result: +32R (my best month, and I was wrong more often than right)

The data was crystal clear: my average R was a much better predictor of profitability than my win rate. And it also told me what to fix.

When my average R dropped below 1.0 in Month 2, I knew something was wrong with my exits. I was taking profits too early.

## How to Start Tracking R-Multiples

It's simpler than you think.

**Step 1: Add three fields to your trade log**
- Entry Price
- Stop Loss
- Actual Exit

**Step 2: Calculate R for each trade**
```
R = (Exit - Entry) / (Entry - Stop)
```

**Step 3: Track your average R over 20+ trades**
You need at least 20 trades for the data to be meaningful. 50+ is better.

**Step 4: Optimize based on the data**
- Average R above 1.0? Your strategy works. Keep doing what you're doing.
- Average R below 0.8? Your exits are too small or your losses are too big. Fix it.
- Average R between 0.8 and 1.0? You're borderline. Optimize.

## The Hard Part: Setting Targets Based on R

Here's what most traders get wrong: they set targets in dollars instead of R.

**Wrong way:**
"I'll make $200 on this trade."

**Right way:**
"My risk on this trade is 20 cents per share × 200 shares = $40 (1R). My target should be at least 1R ($40 profit = 60 cent move), preferably 2R ($80 profit = $1 move)."

When you think in R-multiples, position sizing becomes automatic:
- Risk 1% of your account per trade
- If your account is $5,000, risk $50 per trade (1R = $50)
- Target should be minimum 1R ($50 profit), ideally 2R ($100)

## Common Objections

**"But I don't use stop losses!"**
Then you don't know your risk. You can't calculate R without a defined stop. Start using stops. Your account will thank you.

**"My strategy is different. I trade without targets."**
Then you have no idea what your R-multiple is. You're flying blind. Add a target. Even if you exit early, you'll see what your strategy ACTUALLY delivers vs. what you THINK it delivers.

**"R-multiples are too complicated."**
The formula is 4 numbers divided by each other. If that's too complicated, trading is too complicated.

## The Bottom Line

Win rate is like checking your speed while driving. It tells you how fast you're going, but not where you're headed.

R-multiple is your GPS. It tells you if you're going the right direction.

Start tracking it today. Not tomorrow. Not "when you find the right tool." Today. Even if it's a notebook.

After 20 trades, calculate your average R. If it's above 1.0, your strategy works. If it's below, your exits need work.

That's the whole game.
