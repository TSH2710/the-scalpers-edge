## What Is a Wash Sale?

A wash sale occurs when you:

1. Sell a stock at a loss
2. Buy the same (or substantially identical) stock within 30 days BEFORE or AFTER the sale
3. That loss is disallowed for tax purposes

The IRS doesn't let you take the loss deduction. Instead, the disallowed loss gets added to the cost basis of the new shares.

**Example:**

- Monday: Sell 100 shares of XYZ at $50 for a $200 loss
- Thursday: Buy 100 shares of XYZ at $48
- Result: The $200 loss is disallowed. Your cost basis on the new shares becomes $50 ($48 purchase + $2 disallowed loss).

If you sell the new shares for a gain, you'll pay taxes on that gain PLUS the disallowed loss (because it's baked into your cost basis). You don't lose the loss forever — you just defer it.

But here's the problem for day traders: **we trigger wash sales constantly.**

## Why Day Traders Get Wrecked by Wash Sales

Swing traders might trigger a wash sale once a quarter. Day traders trigger them every week.

Here's a typical week for me:

| Day | Trade | Result |
|-----|-------|--------|
| Monday | Buy XYZ @ $50, sell @ $49 | -$100 loss |
| Tuesday | Buy XYZ @ $48.50, sell @ $49.50 | +$100 gain |
| Wednesday | Buy XYZ @ $49, sell @ $48.50 | -$50 loss |

That Wednesday loss? It's a wash sale. Because I bought XYZ within 30 days of the Monday loss.

The $50 loss gets added to my cost basis on the Wednesday shares. When I eventually close those shares, I'll pay tax on the gain PLUS the $50 disallowed loss.

**Multiply this across 15 trades per day, 5 days per week, 52 weeks per year.** Now you see the problem.

## The Numbers: How Much This Costs You

Let's say you're a moderately active scalper:

- 15 trades/day × 5 days/week = 75 trades/week
- Average loss per trade: $80
- 35% of trades are losses = ~26 losing trades/week
- Estimated wash sales: ~30-40% of those = 8-10 wash sales/week
- 8 wash sales × $80 average loss = ~$640/week in disallowed losses
- Over 50 trading weeks: ~$32,000 in deferred losses

That's not money you're paying extra in taxes. That's money you CAN'T deduct now — it's tied up in your cost basis.

The real cost? **Cash flow.** You're paying taxes on gains you haven't fully accounted for, and you can't use those losses to offset other income.

## How to Track Wash Sales

Most brokerages will generate a wash sale report for you at year-end. But by then it's too late to do anything about it.

Here's what I track in my journal:

### 1. Every Loss Trade
Log every losing trade with:
- Date of sale
- Ticker
- Number of shares
- Loss amount
- Loss per share

### 2. Every Buy Within 30 Days
When I buy a stock I've sold at a loss within the past 30 days, I flag it immediately.

### 3. The Calculation
For each flagged trade:
```
Wash sale disallowed = Loss per share × Shares rebought
New cost basis = Purchase price + Disallowed loss per share
```

### 4. The Calendar
I mark wash sale windows on my calendar. If I sell AAPL at a loss on March 1st, I know I can't buy AAPL again until March 31st (30 days).

## Strategies to Avoid Wash Sales

### Strategy 1: Wait 31 Days
The simplest approach. After selling at a loss, don't rebuy the same stock for 31 days.

For swing traders, this is easy. For scalpers who trade the same names daily, this is brutal.

### Strategy 2: Use a Substitute Stock
The IRS rule says "substantially identical" stock. There's some gray area here.

If you sell AAPL at a loss, can you buy QQQ (which holds AAPL)? The IRS has said no to this for ETFs that track the same index.

But some traders use sector ETFs as substitutes. Sell AAPL, buy XLK (tech sector ETF). It's not identical, but it's correlated.

**Important:** I'm not a tax professional. Consult one before using this strategy. The IRS has been cracking down on wash sale avoidance.

### Strategy 3: Track It and Accept It
If you trade the same stocks daily, wash sales are inevitable. The strategy isn't to avoid them — it's to track them and understand your true cost basis.

At year-end:
1. Add up all your wash sale disallowances
2. Add them to your cost basis
3. When you finally close those positions, the losses will come through
4. You can use them to offset gains in future years

This is the most realistic approach for day traders.

## Wash Sales and Tax-Loss Harvesting

There's an upside to wash sales if you plan ahead.

**Tax-loss harvesting** is when you intentionally sell at a loss to offset gains. For day traders, this happens naturally.

Here's the strategy:
1. At year-end, identify positions with large unrealized losses
2. Sell them to realize the loss (if you haven't already)
3. Don't rebuy for 31 days
4. Use the loss to offset your short-term gains (which are taxed at ordinary income rates)

For scalpers, short-term gains are EVERYTHING. Every wash sale loss you can deduct reduces your taxable income.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Not tracking losses at all**
If you only track winning trades, you have no idea what your wash sale exposure is. Track every trade. Wins and losses.

**Mistake 2: Rebuying without checking the calendar**
Before buying any stock, check: did I sell this at a loss in the past 30 days? If yes, flag it.

**Mistake 3: Forgetting "substantially identical"**
It's not just the same ticker. It's also options on the same stock, ETFs tracking the same index, and in some cases, stocks in the same sector.

**Mistake 4: Waiting until tax time**
Your brokerage will send you a 1099-B, but it might not include every wash sale detail. Especially if you have multiple accounts. Track it yourself throughout the year.
