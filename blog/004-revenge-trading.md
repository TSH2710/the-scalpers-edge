# How to Stop Revenge Trading After a Bad Loss

*The one trading psychology problem that costs scalpers more money than any strategy mistake — and the simple rule that fixes it.*

---

I want you to think about your worst trading day last month.

Now think about your worst trade from that day. The one where you lost the most money.

I'm willing to bet it wasn't a trade you planned. It wasn't a trade where you followed your checklist. It was a trade you took because you were angry, frustrated, or desperate.

That's revenge trading. And it's probably costing you more than any strategy flaw.

## What Revenge Trading Actually Is

Revenge trading is when you take a trade — not because the setup is there, but because you want to make back a previous loss.

It's emotional. It's impulsive. And it's expensive.

Here's the thing: everyone does it. I did it for the first months of my trading. I just didn't have a name for it.

## What the Numbers Look Like When You Track It

When you log your emotional state before every trade for a couple of months, the revenge-trade data tends to look something like this:

**Roughly 1 in 5 trades gets flagged as a revenge trade**
**Average R on revenge trades: around -2R**
**Average R on planned trades: around +0.7R**

That's a 2.7R swing per trade — the difference between your best habit and your worst one.

Translate it to dollars at $50 risk per trade (1R = $50):

- **Planned trade average: +$35**
- **Revenge trade average: -$100**
- **Difference: $135 per trade**

Run that across a few revenge trades a week and you're leaking a four-figure amount per quarter. Because you couldn't sit still for 5 minutes.

Your numbers will be your own — that's exactly why you want them in a journal instead of a guess.

## The Pattern I Didn't See (Until I Started Journaling)

Revenge trading doesn't look like what you think it looks like.

It's not always immediately after a loss. Sometimes it's:
- 30 minutes after a loss (you've "calmed down" but you haven't)
- The next trade (you're determined to "start fresh")
- Later in the day (you've been green but a loss triggered the "make it back" mindset)
- The next morning (you're thinking about yesterday's loss)

The common thread: **the trade is motivated by a previous loss, not by a current setup.**

## Why Your Brain Does This

Your brain doesn't like losing. Evolutionarily, loss = danger. When you lose money, your brain interprets it the same way it interprets a physical threat: fight, flight, or freeze.

In trading, "fight" becomes "trade more to win back what I lost."

It's a normal biological response. But it's a terrible trading strategy.

## The Simple Rule That Fixed It

I tried everything. Meditation. Breathing exercises. Taking a walk between losses. Nothing worked consistently.

Then I tried the simplest possible solution: **a hard rule with zero exceptions.**

### The Rule: Three Consecutive Losses = Day Over

After three losses in a row, I'm done. Not four. Not five. Three.

Here's why three works:
- One loss is normal. Markets move fast and sometimes you're just wrong.
- Two losses in a row might be a bad streak. Could happen to anyone.
- Three losses in a row? You're almost certainly not trading from a clear head.

The third loss was always the worst. Always emotional. Always avoidable. Check your own three-loss streaks in your log — if you have them, you'll see the same escalation.

## Other Rules That Help

**Rule 1: The 1-Hour Rule**
If you take a loss, you don't trade again for at least 1 hour. Not 10 minutes. Not "just one more." One full hour.

Why? Because your brain needs time to reset. The emotional wave of a loss fades after about 45-60 minutes. Trading during that window is gambling.

**Rule 2: The Daily Loss Limit**
Set a dollar amount that's your maximum loss for the day. Not a percentage of your account — a hard dollar amount. When you hit it, you're done.

Mine is a hard dollar number I set before the open — an amount that stings but doesn't threaten the account. When I hit it, I close everything and walk away. No exceptions.

**Rule 3: The "Why Am I Trading?" Check**
Before every trade (especially after a loss), ask yourself one question:

**"If this trade was in my original plan this morning, would I still take it?"**

If the answer is no, don't take it. That simple.

**Rule 4: No Size Increases After Losses**
After a loss, your next trade is HALF your normal size, not double. If you lose on that trade, you're done for the day.

The temptation is to "make it back" with a bigger position. That's how accounts blow up. Half size forces you to slow down.

## What to Track (The Psychology Tracker)

I added a psychology tracker to my journal that logs:

- Pre-trade mood (calm, anxious, excited, bored, angry)
- Pre-trade energy level (1-10)
- FOMO flag (yes/no)
- Revenge trading flag (yes/no)
- Overconfidence flag (yes/no)
- Post-trade emotional state

After a few weeks, the patterns became obvious. My worst trades were almost always flagged as revenge or FOMO.

Now I have a system. The flags don't prevent me from taking bad trades — but they make me AWARE of when I'm in a bad state. That awareness is half the battle.

## The Truth About Trading Psychology

Here's what nobody tells you about trading psychology:

**You don't "fix" it. You manage it.**

There's no point where you're completely immune to revenge trading. There's no meditation technique that makes you unemotional. The goal isn't to eliminate the feeling — it's to prevent the feeling from turning into a trade.

Rules are how you do that. Not willpower. Rules.

Willpower is finite. It runs out after a bad day, a fight with your partner, a bad night's sleep. Rules don't run out.

## For the Scalpers Specifically

Scalpers are particularly vulnerable to revenge trading because:

1. **The feedback loop is fast.** You can lose money and try to make it back within minutes.
2. **The FOMO is real.** You see a stock running and think "I missed it, now I need to catch it."
3. **The session is short.** You feel like you have to "make hay while the sun shines" — even if you're already down.
4. **There's always another setup.** This sounds good, but it's also the problem. You can always find a reason to trade.

The rules above (3 losses = done, 1-hour rule, daily loss limit) are specifically designed for scalpers who need hard boundaries.

## My Current Routine

Here's what my trading day looks like now:

**Before the open:**
- Pre-trade checklist (2 minutes)
- Set my daily loss limit
- Confirm I'm in the right headspace

**During the session:**
- Trade my plan
- Log every trade immediately
- If I hit 2 losses: half size next trade
- If I hit 3 losses: day over. Done.

**After the session:**
- Weekly review on Friday
- Look for patterns in my psychology data
- Adjust rules if needed

This routine hasn't made me a genius. It stopped the self-sabotage — which, for a first year, is the whole game.

## The Bottom Line

You know how I said your win rate doesn't matter? Well, neither does your strategy if you're revenge trading.

The best setup in the world becomes a bad trade when you're trading from anger.

Start tracking your emotional state. Set hard rules. Enforce them ruthlessly.

Your future self — the one with a funded account and consistent profits — will thank you.
